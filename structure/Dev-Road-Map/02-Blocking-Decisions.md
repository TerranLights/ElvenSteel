# Blocking Decisions

Decisions that must be resolved before substantive work can proceed in the areas they affect. None of these are questions with an obvious right answer — they are choices that have been left open, and leaving them open would mean building downstream work on undefined ground.

**What qualifies as a blocking decision (not just an open question):** something a later phase genuinely cannot proceed past without an answer. Typical sources for a novel:
- An ambiguous chapter split or merge in a hand-written outline (which chapter does this stanza actually belong to?)
- A character *identity* question — is this a new character, or an existing one appearing under a different label? (This is different from a character simply not having a proper name assigned yet — see the note below.)
- A structural choice with real downstream consequences (POV, tense, whether a subplot is kept or cut)

**What does NOT qualify as a blocking decision:**
- **A character not yet having a proper name.** Personality, arc, and role can be fully designed under a placeholder/shorthand identifier — naming is settled whenever convenient (see `03-Phase-1-Foundations.md`, `04-Phase-2-Characters.md`) and never gates character or plot work.
- **Theme not yet being clear.** Theme is discovered from finished or partially-finished plot/character/structure work, not designed in advance — see `07-Phase-5-Theme.md`. It is never logged here as blocking.

Make these before moving to Phase 1.

## Decision 1: Where the elves come from

**Status: PARTLY RESOLVED (author, 2026-10-30)**

**Settled:** the elves come from **our Earth** and escaped to the parallel Earth through a stargate.

**Still open:**
- **Why they left.** The author's original thought was increasing hostility from humans; they are open to better options.
- **Where and when the aliens engineered the elves** (and the orcs), given the elves come from our Earth. Related: `Hybrid-Anthro_Culture` Q-5, on what the elves were engineered from.

**Conflicts with canon:** the Timeline's `Reference/Doll_Representation_Categories.md` says Category 3 elves were "genetically engineered by aliens, on a parallel Earth." That line needs updating. It is marked for review, and any Timeline edit needs the author's instruction.

**Downstream effects:** the elves' history and culture, the stargates' history, and why an Irishman can stumble through one.

## Decision 2: Elven Steel's place in the Dolliverse

**Status: OPEN, in part.** Settled (author, 2026-10-30): **Elly was built by aliens.**

The Doll categories file defines robots as "originally built by humans; later came to be built by each other." Elly was built by aliens. If Elven Steel belongs to the same Dolliverse, does that definition need an exception, or is this parallel Earth a separate case? Related: `Hybrid-Anthro_Culture` Q-11 (Elven Steel and the multiverse), Q-1 (what category the orcs are) and Q-20 (Selandriel as the first category-3 character).

**Downstream effects:** Elly's origin story and the canon files the series must agree with.

## Decision 3: The way into space

**Status: RESOLVED (author, 2026-10-30)**

**The way off the planet** is a technology that already exists on the parallel Earth, **inside a compound the orcs have taken over.** By sheer fortune, the orcs haven't yet worked out how to operate it, but they are **dangerously close** to doing so.

**Still open:**
- what the technology is, and who left it there;
- how Elly's sacrifice gets her family and tribe through it.

Elly's descent itself is still one-way, with no obvious way back up into orbit.

**Downstream effects:** the finale, and setups across the season (see `../things to introduce.md`).

## Decision 4: Arc structure for the elves

**Status: RESOLVED (author, 2026-10-30)**

**Major elf characters** use the author's expanded (composite) structure. **Minor elf characters** use a basic Save the Cat structure, as Save the Cat was originally intended.

Selandriel's and Rhylanna's arc files now use the expanded template. The Irishman, a human, keeps the Harmon circle.

**Downstream effects:** how deep the elves' arcs go, and how many beats each episode carries.
